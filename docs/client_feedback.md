# Client Feedback and Redesign Brief
## Cedar & Stone Home Services

**Website under review:** https://husson-webdesign.github.io/amury-P7-CedarAndStone/

Hello,

Thank you for sharing the first version of our website. I appreciate that it introduces our services, provides a way to explore them, includes a phone link, and already tells visitors that the estimate form is a demonstration. The three service categories—deck maintenance, carpentry repairs, and seasonal home maintenance—are a helpful starting point.

For the next version, I would like a much more complete redesign. As a home services business, our website needs to make a homeowner feel that we are organized, dependable, and easy to work with. Right now, the site feels like an early draft: all the content is on one page, our name is inconsistent, the layout does not yet show the character of our business, and an image in the welcome section did not load when I viewed it.

Please use the requests below as the direction for the next version. I want a professional, welcoming website with separate pages, a recognizable identity, clear service information, and an honest demonstration of how a customer would prepare an estimate request.

## Phase 1: Establish Our Business Identity

### 1. Confirm and use one business name

The website currently uses **“Cedar & Home Services”** in the header, main heading, and footer, while the About section says **“Cedar & Stone Home Services.”** Please check the project brief and confirm our intended name with me. Once confirmed, use it consistently in the logo, page headings, browser tabs, footer, and throughout the content.

For this feedback, I will refer to the business as **Cedar & Stone Home Services**, but please verify that name before finalizing it.

### 2. Create a recognizable brand

I would like our identity to feel dependable, established, and connected to the kind of work we do. The current green and gold colors could be a starting point, but the site needs a more deliberate and memorable presentation.

Please develop:

- A clear, professional wordmark or simple logo suitable for our business.
- A coordinated color palette that feels appropriate for home repairs and maintenance.
- A readable combination of headings and body text.
- Consistent buttons, image treatments, spacing, and page layouts.
- A short tagline that explains our value without making claims we cannot support.

The result should look like one business across every page, and it should still be easy to read on a phone.

### 3. Give the website a stronger purpose

The current headline repeats our name and the line “Practical home repairs. Dependable local service.” I would like the opening to explain more clearly **what we help with, who we serve, and the area we serve**.

For example, a visitor should understand that we help homeowners and landlords with the types of repair and maintenance projects listed in the project. Use the approved service area from the project brief; ask me if it is unclear.

## Phase 2: Rebuild the Website as Separate Pages

### 4. Create a complete, multipage website

The current navigation moves visitors between sections of one long page. For this redesign, please create separate pages so each part of the business has room to be useful and easy to find.

I would like at least these pages:

1. **Home** — Introduce the business, highlight services, and guide visitors to the next step.
2. **About** — Explain our story, approach, and the customers we serve.
3. **Services** — Show and explain the available service categories.
4. **Contact / Estimate** — Display approved contact details and the demonstration estimate form.

You may also create a **Projects or Gallery** page and a **Frequently Asked Questions** page or section if you have enough approved material. Each page should feel complete, have a clear purpose, and be reachable from the main navigation.

### 5. Create a helpful homepage

Please redesign the homepage to include:

- A strong opening message and relevant image.
- A short introduction to the business and the people we help.
- A preview of our three service categories with links to more details.
- A simple explanation of how a customer would get started.
- A brief reason to consider our business, based only on information we can support.
- A clear invitation to explore services or prepare an estimate inquiry.

The homepage should provide a useful overview without trying to contain every detail from the other pages.

### 6. Develop the About page

Please explain why the business exists, what types of projects we help with, and how we approach working with homeowners and landlords. The existing text about handling smaller repairs and maintenance tasks is a useful starting point.

If the project does not provide the owner's background, years of experience, insurance, licensing, staff details, or guarantees, please ask me for those details. Do not invent them.

### 7. Develop the Services page

Please give each of the existing services enough room to be understood:

- **Deck maintenance** — Include the approved description of minor repairs, board replacement, inspection, cleaning, and seasonal preparation.
- **Carpentry repairs** — Explain the approved work involving trim, doors, railings, shelving, and similar residential woodwork.
- **Seasonal home maintenance** — Explain the approved purpose of preparing a property for changing Maine weather.

For each service, include an appropriate image, a short description, examples of work where confirmed, and a way to prepare an inquiry. Clearly explain any limitations or exclusions if they are provided. Do not add plumbing, electrical, structural work, or other regulated or specialized services without confirmation.

### 8. Develop the Contact / Estimate page

Create a dedicated page that brings together the approved business contact information, operating hours, service area, and demonstration form. Explain what information a homeowner should prepare when describing a project.

Please check whether the displayed address, phone number, email address, operating hours, and free-estimate offer are approved project details or placeholders. The current `.example` email and `555` phone number look like demonstration details. Label fictional details appropriately and do not replace them with invented ones.

## Phase 3: Improve the Visual Design and Layout

### 9. Replace the unfinished-looking opening

The current opening is a dark green block with a grid background and a large area that does not communicate much about our work. Please redesign it so the first screen immediately feels like a professional home services company.

I would like a balanced combination of clear text, a relevant image, and well-placed actions. The headline should be easy to read and the visitor should know where to go next without scrolling or guessing.

### 10. Fix the image that did not load

When I viewed the welcome section, the image did not appear; only its description was visible. Please fix the image or replace it with a suitable one, and check the published site to make sure it loads.

The current description refers to a technician repairing a leaking sink, while the listed services focus on deck maintenance, carpentry, and seasonal maintenance. Please choose an image that matches the work we actually offer, unless plumbing is confirmed as a service.

### 11. Use meaningful, consistent imagery

Select images that help visitors understand our work, such as deck maintenance, carpentry, and seasonal property care. Make sure images:

- Are clear and appropriately sized.
- Fit their spaces without stretching.
- Can be legally used for this project, with sources documented.
- Are described appropriately for visitors who cannot see them.
- Are not presented as our completed projects unless their origin is confirmed.

If actual project photos are unavailable, you may use permitted illustrative photos and identify them honestly. Do not create fake before-and-after results or customer testimonials.

### 12. Improve the layout throughout the site

Please establish a consistent content width, clear alignment, and comfortable spacing. Use distinct sections and attractive service cards so that visitors can scan the information easily.

Make headings, descriptions, images, and buttons feel connected. The About and Contact pages should have their own considered layouts rather than looking like copied blocks from the homepage.

### 13. Build a useful footer

The current footer contains only the copyright line. Please provide a more useful footer with the confirmed business name, a short description, key page links, approved contact information, and operating hours where appropriate. Keep it organized and readable on small screens.

## Phase 4: Navigation and Customer Journey

### 14. Make navigation clear and consistent

Keep the navigation choices understandable and consistent across all pages. The business name or logo should take visitors to the homepage, and the current page should be easy to identify.

Include a clear route to the estimate demonstration from every page. The site already has a skip-to-content link; please retain it and ensure it leads to the correct content on each page.

### 15. Make the next step clear

A homeowner should be able to follow a simple journey:

1. Learn what work we offer.
2. Choose the service that fits their project.
3. Prepare the details for an estimate inquiry.

Add relevant links between pages. If a customer chooses a particular service, it would be helpful for that service to be selected on the inquiry form, with the option to change it.

### 16. Use accurate action labels

The current form says “Send Request,” even though the notice explains that it is a demonstration. Please use wording such as **“Preview My Request”** so the action matches what the visitor can actually do. Similarly, do not imply that an estimate has been received, a visit has been scheduled, or work has been booked.

## Phase 5: Responsive Design and Accessibility

### 17. Make the site work well on phones and tablets

Homeowners may look at the site while walking around their property. Please test the redesigned pages at several screen sizes and make sure:

- The navigation is easy to open and use.
- Text can be read without zooming.
- Images, cards, and forms fit the screen.
- Buttons and links are easy to tap.
- The content does not require sideways scrolling.
- The customer can reach the estimate demonstration from a phone.

### 18. Make the website welcoming to more visitors

Please make sure visitors can use the site with a keyboard, see which link or control is selected, understand the form labels, and read text against its background. Images should have useful descriptions when they convey information.

If an interactive menu, filter, or question-and-answer section is added, it should be easy to operate and its open or selected state should be clear. Test these features rather than relying only on their appearance.

## Phase 6: Estimate Demonstration and Useful Interactivity

### 19. Improve the demonstration form

I appreciate that the current form states it does not send information. Please make that notice visible before the form is completed, and clarify that the demonstration does not send or save the request.

When a visitor completes the required information, show an attractive summary of the service and project details they entered. Allow them to return and make changes. Give clear guidance when required information is missing or invalid.

The form must not say that the business received a request, that an estimate was issued, or that a consultation was booked. No email service, backend, or actual submission is required for this project.

### 20. Help customers find the right service

Please add a simple way to explore the services, such as selecting a category or choosing the type of project. Show a useful result and let the visitor return to all services. If no service matches, provide a friendly message and suggest the “Other / Not sure” choice in the demonstration form.

An optional questions-and-answers section could also help customers prepare. Please use only confirmed information and identify unanswered questions for me.

### 21. Review the business-hours indicator

The contact section currently shows an “Open now” message. If we keep it, please verify that it reflects the approved hours and the business's local time, including changes such as daylight saving time. If the approved hours or reliable behavior cannot be confirmed, show the static hours instead of an inaccurate live status.

## Phase 7: Make Service Information Easier to Maintain

### 22. Organize the services in JSON

I would like future updates to service descriptions and images to be easier for a developer to make. Please keep our service information together in a separate **JSON file** and use it to display the services on the website.

Please make sure:

- Deck maintenance, carpentry repairs, and seasonal home maintenance are included.
- Each service contains its approved name, description, image information, and inquiry option.
- The service information displayed on the website matches the file.
- The service search, category selector, or filter uses that information.
- Visitors see a helpful message if the information cannot be displayed.
- A future developer can find the file and understand how to update it using the project documentation.

You may extend the same idea to approved project examples or questions and answers if time permits. Please do not add unconfirmed prices, credentials, guarantees, or services.

## Phase 8: Final Quality Review and Handoff

### 23. Check the entire published website

Before presenting the redesign, please check:

- Every page opens directly and can be reached through the navigation.
- The business name and approved details are consistent everywhere.
- Every link, button, image, and service choice works on the published site.
- No image is missing or distorted.
- The form clearly states that it is a demonstration and does not send or save information.
- The JSON-powered service information loads on GitHub Pages.
- Pages work on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, text readability, and image descriptions are checked.
- Spelling and grammar are reviewed.
- Browser tabs clearly identify the business and each page.

Please provide:

1. The published website link.
2. The project repository link.
3. A brief explanation of the redesign and the purpose of each page.
4. Desktop and phone screenshots.
5. A list of information that still needs my confirmation.

8. A short testing checklist showing what you reviewed.
